If you've called around Omaha for RV cleaning quotes, you've probably noticed something: nobody wants to tell you a number until they've seen your rig. We think that's backwards. Here's what RV cleaning actually costs in the Omaha metro, and why.

## Typical Omaha-area pricing

- **Exterior wash:** $12 per foot — roughly $290–$480 for a 24–40 foot RV
- **The Signature (exterior + interior):** $30 per foot — roughly $720–$1,200, including dry-vapor steam sanitation
- **Focused mattress, oxidation, and coach steam work:** available as quoted add-ons

These match the national picture: most RV owners across the country pay somewhere between $150 and $500 for routine cleaning, and $1,000+ for full restoration work on large motorhomes.

## What moves the price

**Length.** The single biggest factor. A 20-foot bumper pull is a half-day job; a 40-foot Class A with a full-body paint job is a serious undertaking.

**Condition.** Black streaks that have baked on for seasons, algae on the roof, or an interior that's been closed up all winter all add time.

**Roof type.** Rubber (TPO/EPDM) roofs need different products and a gentler process than fiberglass.

**Access.** Driveway, storage lot, or campground — all fine for us since our rig is self-contained, but a spot where we can work all the way around speeds things up.

## What "too cheap" looks like

A $99 "full RV detail" in a metro market isn't a deal — it's a car-wash crew treating your gel coat like a Corolla. Brushed washes scratch, dish soap strips any wax you had, and nobody's getting on the roof at that price. Your RV is likely the second-most-expensive thing you own; it deserves specialist care at a fair price.

## The honest answer on value

The best money most owners spend is a **seasonal schedule**: a fall storage wash, a spring freshen-up, and one or two mid-season washes. That routine prevents the oxidation and staining that lead to the four-figure correction jobs later. We offer a season pass that discounts exactly that.
