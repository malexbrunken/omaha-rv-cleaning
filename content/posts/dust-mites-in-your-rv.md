If you wake up in your RV with congestion, itchy eyes, or a scratchy throat that clears up an hour after you're up — and nobody else in the house has a cold — you're probably not allergic to camping. You're allergic to your mattress.

## Why RVs are dust mite paradise

Dust mites need three things: fabric to live in, food (your shed skin cells), and humidity. An RV bedroom delivers all three in a small sealed box:

- **The mattress never gets replaced or flipped** like a house mattress tends to
- **Every night adds food and moisture** from sleepers
- **Humid storage** — a coach closed up in a Nebraska summer or damp autumn is a terrarium
- **No deep cleaning ever happens** — most cleaning companies won't touch RV mattresses because wet methods ruin the foam

## What the research says about killing them

Dust mites die at temperatures above roughly 130°F. That's why washing bedding hot works — but you can't put a mattress through the wash. The two proven in-place methods are allergen-encasement covers (which trap but don't remove) and heat. Which brings us to:

## Dry vapor steam

Dry vapor steam delivers 300°F+ at the nozzle with under 5% moisture — hot enough to kill mites, their eggs, and the bacteria feeding on the same debris, without soaking the foam core that can't be dried inside a closed coach. It's the method used in hospital and hotel mattress hygiene for exactly this reason.
